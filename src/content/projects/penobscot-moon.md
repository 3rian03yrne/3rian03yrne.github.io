The Penobscot calendar is lunisolar. A year runs from the longest, moonless night of one winter to the next, every new Moon starts a month of roughly 29.5 days, and when twelve months are spent before that night comes round again, a thirteenth is added.

So "what Moon is it?" is a harder question than it sounds. Penobscot Moon answers it for any date, and prints the answer onto a label you can stick on a jar.

## Features

- Today's Moon name in Penobscot and English, the moment you open the app
- Any date you pick, past or future, so you can label a batch you put up last season
- Moon phase and percent illuminated shown alongside the name
- Printable label sheets — twenty 2x2 inch labels to a page — for canning and foraging jars
- A custom message on each label, or leave the notes box blank and handwrite it
- The thirteenth moon handled for you in the years that have one
- Works offline once installed, so it still answers you off-grid

## How it works

The calendar runs on the device, so there is no network call after install. Pick a date and the app works the arithmetic from it: which lunar month that date falls in, how many days into it you are, and how much of the Moon was lit that night.

## About

My wife has labelled her foraging and canning jars with the Penobscot name of the Moon for as long as I have known her. I found her at the kitchen table one day, mid-batch and stalled, trying to work out which Moon it actually was — and I couldn't tell her. So I built this instead.

It was a gift, which is why it is free and always will be.
